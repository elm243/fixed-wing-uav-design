\# Aircraft Configuration Trade Study



\## 1. Purpose



The purpose of this trade study is to evaluate candidate fixed-wing aircraft configurations and select a baseline configuration for the UAV project.



The selected configuration should best support the mission and system requirements defined in the Mission Definition and Requirements documents.



\---



\## 2. Candidate Configurations



The following aircraft configurations will be evaluated:



1\. High-Wing Conventional Tractor

2\. Mid-Wing Conventional Tractor

3\. High-Wing Conventional Pusher

4\. Mid-Wing Conventional Pusher

5\. Twin-Boom Pusher

6\. Pod-and-Boom Tractor

7\. Flying Wing



Each configuration will be evaluated using the same weighted decision criteria.



\---



\## 3. Evaluation Criteria



The following criteria were selected based on the UAV mission and project requirements.



| Criterion | Weight | Rationale |

|---|---:|---|

| \*\*Aerodynamic Efficiency at High Altitude\*\* | 25% | Overall aerodynamic and endurance potential near the 15,000 ft design altitude, including drag, lift-to-drag performance, and suitability for efficient flight in lower-density air. |

| \*\*Flight Stability and Control\*\* | 20% | How naturally stable, predictable, and controllable the configuration is throughout the expected flight envelope. |

| \*\*Payload Integration\*\* | 20% | How effectively the configuration can accommodate modular payloads while maintaining accessibility, useful internal volume, and acceptable center-of-gravity location. |

| \*\*Structural Simplicity / Manufacturability\*\* | 15% | How practical the configuration is to design, construct, align, repair, and modify. |

| \*\*Recovery and Ground Handling\*\* | 10% | How well the configuration supports takeoff, landing, recovery, propeller protection, and repeated operation. |

| \*\*Systems Integration / Growth\*\* | 10% | How easily batteries, avionics, autopilot hardware, telemetry, wiring, and future systems can be installed, accessed, and expanded. |



\*\*Total Weight: 100%\*\*



\---



\## 4. Scoring Method



Each configuration will receive a score from 1 to 5 for every evaluation criterion.



\- \*\*1 — Poor\*\*

\- \*\*2 — Below Average\*\*

\- \*\*3 — Acceptable\*\*

\- \*\*4 — Good\*\*

\- \*\*5 — Excellent\*\*



The weighted score for each criterion is calculated as:



\*\*Weighted Score = Score × Criterion Weight\*\*



Criterion weights are expressed as decimals in the calculation. For example, a criterion weighted at 25% uses a value of 0.25.



The weighted scores for all criteria will then be summed to determine the overall score for each configuration. The configuration with the highest overall weighted score will be considered the preferred baseline configuration.

\### 6.7 Flying Wing



| Criterion | Score | Justification |

|---|---:|---|

| Aerodynamic Efficiency at High Altitude | \*\*5\*\* | The flying-wing configuration eliminates the conventional fuselage and tail, reducing wetted area and parasitic drag. This gives it strong potential for high lift-to-drag ratio and efficient long-duration flight, making it especially attractive for high-altitude endurance missions. |

| Flight Stability and Control | \*\*2\*\* | A flying wing lacks a conventional horizontal and vertical tail, making longitudinal and directional stability more difficult to achieve. | Payload Integration | \*\*3\*\* | Internal payload volume can be substantial, but it is spread across the wing rather than concentrated in a conventional fuselage. Payload placement is more sensitive to center-of-gravity location, and accommodating bulky or modular payloads can be difficult without disturbing the aerodynamic shape. |

| Structural Simplicity / Manufacturability | \*\*3\*\* | The configuration uses fewer major external components than a conventional aircraft, but the wing must carry aerodynamic, structural, payload, and control functions simultaneously. Maintaining aerodynamic shape, structural stiffness, and internal volume can make construction more demanding than the simple appearance suggests. |

| Recovery and Ground Handling | \*\*3\*\* | The lack of a conventional tail reduces some landing-damage risks and can support belly landing, but the broad wing planform and exposed leading edges can be vulnerable during rough recovery. Ground handling and launch methods may also require more specialized consideration. |

| Systems Integration / Growth | \*\*3\*\* | Avionics and batteries can be distributed within the wing, but internal depth is limited and component placement is strongly constrained by center-of-gravity requirements and wing structure. \## 7. Results



\### Selected Configuration



\*\*Configuration:\*\* High-Wing Conventional Pusher



\*\*Total Weighted Score:\*\* 4.25



\### Selection Rationale



The High-Wing Conventional Pusher was selected as the baseline configuration because it achieved the highest weighted score and provided the best overall balance of mission-relevant characteristics.



The high-wing arrangement offers favorable stability and ground-handling characteristics, while the pusher propulsion layout leaves the nose unobstructed for modular payloads, sensors, and future mission equipment. The configuration also provides strong systems-integration potential while remaining relatively conventional and practical to manufacture.



Although it did not receive the highest possible score for aerodynamic efficiency at high altitude, its combined performance in flight stability, payload integration, manufacturability, recovery, and future system growth made it the strongest overall choice for the current UAV mission.



\---



\## 8. Sensitivity and Limitations



The results of this trade study depend on the selected criteria, weighting factors, and preliminary engineering assumptions. Because this is an early concept-level trade study, several evaluations are qualitative and may change as more detailed design data becomes available.



The selected configuration may be reevaluated if later aerodynamic analysis, structural analysis, propulsion sizing, or mission requirements indicate that another configuration provides a significant advantage.
